# Allocard product demo

Target length: 2 minutes 50 seconds

## Creative direction

Bright, clean, and confident. Open with the Allocard brand, then let the product fill the screen.

Two phones represent the employer and employee. Use them to make the relationship clear: the employer sets the budget, the employee spends, and both see the result. Move between full interface views and close-ups of the moments that matter.

The narration explains the value. The interface shows it happening. Background text names the features in a few words. No setup instructions or click-by-click explanations.

## 0:00–0:12 — Meet Allocard

**Voiceover**

“Meet Allocard. A better way for teams to manage company spending in crypto. Give employees the freedom to spend, while keeping control of company funds.”

**Visual**

The Allocard logo appears on a bright background. Dashboard details come into view: treasury balance, employee allowances, and the delegation tree. Pull back to reveal the product inside two phones.

**Background text**

Your team can spend.

You stay in control.

## 0:12–0:30 — Why it exists

**Voiceover**

“Small teams need every part of their budget to count. Money sitting in employee accounts could be needed elsewhere. But waiting for a transfer before every expense slows everyone down.

Allocard keeps the money in your treasury and gives employees permission to spend when they need to.”

**Visual**

A treasury balance separates into employee balances. Some remain unused. Bring the funds back into one treasury, connected to the employee phone by a spending allowance.

**Background text**

Keep funds together.

Keep work moving.

## 0:30–1:10 — Spending, connected

**Voiceover**

“From one dashboard, a company can give an employee an allowance and set the rules around it.

The employee can pay directly from the treasury, within those limits. Every payment appears in the company’s activity, so the employer can see where the money goes.

Employees can also delegate part of their allowance to an AI agent. Each agent works within a smaller permission, governed by the employee’s own limits.

Discover agents through NEAR AI Market. Bring in a purchasing research agent to compare tools, check prices, and help your team choose.”

**Visual**

Both phones stay on screen, labelled **Employer** and **Employee**.

An allowance appears on the employer side and connects to the employee side. The employee makes a payment; its confirmation appears, followed by the activity entry on the employer phone.

Extend the connection to an agent. Show the allowance hierarchy, marketplace agent cards, and a finished research comparison. Use quick, readable cuts rather than following the full interaction.

**Background text**

Company → Employee → Agent

Delegate. Spend. See what happens.

## 1:10–1:30 — Built for smaller teams

**Voiceover**

“Built for crypto-native teams and businesses that pay expenses onchain, Allocard gives you flexibility without losing oversight.

You do not have to fund each employee’s account in advance or approve every routine payment. Set their allowance, keep the money together, and reclaim the unused budget when priorities change.”

**Visual**

The two phones move aside to frame the treasury. Highlight allocated and available funds. An unused allowance is revoked, and the available budget updates.

**Background text**

One treasury.

Flexible allowances.

Clear oversight.

There is no separate NEAR wallet to set up. Privy creates an embedded wallet through email or social login.

NEP-366 meta-transactions let Allocard cover gas for sponsored actions. Employees can pay without buying NEAR or managing gas.”

**Visual**

Short interface moments: an employee email becomes a team member; social sign-in opens the dashboard; a payment confirms.

Keep the product prominent. Introduce the technical names as small supporting labels beside the relevant moment.

## 2:10–2:40 — Your rules, enforced

**Voiceover**

“Control starts with the policy. Write what you need in plain English, review the spending rules, and put them to work.

Set total allowances, recurring budgets, payment limits, transaction counts, approved recipients, and expiry dates.

NEAR smart contracts enforce those rules across the delegation chain. An agent cannot receive more authority than its employee has.

And when things change, revoke an employee or agent permission directly onchain. No waiting for a provider to approve it. You decide who can spend, how much, and for how long.”

**Visual**

Show a short policy being typed:

> Give Alex 500 USDC total, up to 100 per week, with a 50 USDC limit per payment.

The text resolves into reviewed policy fields. Highlight other policy controls in quick cuts, with their names appearing behind the phones.

Return to the delegation tree. Show an agent permission revoked, then an employee permission revoked. Their access indicators change, and the company remains at the centre.

**Background text**

Policies in plain English

Recurring budgets · Payment limits · Transaction counts

Recipient controls · Expiry dates

Enforced on NEAR

Revoke access onchain

## Edit rhythm

Use the two phones as the visual thread through the film. Enlarge the active interface when a detail matters, then return to both sides to show the connection.

Let each important result land: an allowance becoming available, a payment confirming, an agent report appearing, access being revoked. Cut the routine work between those moments.

Keep background captions short and show one message at a time. Leave room in the voiceover for the product to speak visually. Record a timed read before locking the edit; use up to three minutes if the interface needs more breathing room.
