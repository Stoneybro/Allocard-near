# Allocard product video

Working draft · 2 minutes 30 seconds

## Concept

A small team needs tools for a new client project. The founder sets a budget, an employee researches options and makes a payment, and the founder stays in control of the money.

Show a few connected moments from that scenario. Cut between the important actions and their results; skip setup screens and the clicks in between.

Main message: **Give your team spending power. Keep control of the money.**

## 0:00–0:15 — Who it helps and why

### Visual

Quick glimpses of the company treasury, an employee allowance, and a successful payment.

### Voiceover

“Small teams that pay expenses in crypto need to keep their money flexible. But employees still need to spend without asking for a transfer every time. Allocard lets them pay from the company treasury, within rules the company sets.”

### On-screen text

**Money stays with the company. Your team keeps moving.**

## 0:15–0:40 — The founder sets the budget

### Visual

Start with a message:

> We need project management software for our new client work.

Show the founder writing a policy:

> Give Alex 500 USDC total, up to 100 per week and 50 per payment, for 30 days.

Cut from the sentence to the resulting limits, then the active allowance in the delegation tree.

### Voiceover

“The founder gives Alex a budget for the project. A few words become spending rules: a total allowance, a weekly limit, and a cap on each payment.”

## 0:40–1:05 — Alex gets help choosing

### Visual

Show Alex giving the research agent a brief:

> Compare project management tools for six people under $50 a month.

Show the smaller agent permission, then cut to the finished report with prices and sources.

### Voiceover

“Alex brings in a research agent to compare options. Its permission sits inside Alex’s allowance, so it cannot receive more authority than Alex has.”

### On-screen note

**Demo agent researches only. No purchases or treasury charges.**

## 1:05–1:30 — Alex makes a payment

### Visual

Move from the report to Alex’s direct payment screen. Show a confirmed testnet payment to a demo recipient, then its appearance in the company activity history.

Label the recipient **Demo vendor · Testnet payment**. This is a demonstration payment, not a purchase from one of the software providers in the report.

### Voiceover

“When it’s time to pay, Alex spends directly from the treasury. There’s no separate transfer to Alex first. Allocard checks the payment against the company’s rules.”

### On-screen text

**Alex signs → Rules checked → Payment confirmed**

## 1:30–1:50 — The founder changes direction

### Visual

A new message appears:

> The client project is paused.

Show the founder reviewing the payment, revoking the allowance, and the remaining budget becoming available again.

### Voiceover

“Plans change. The founder can stop further spending and make the unused allowance available for other work. The money has stayed in the treasury throughout.”

## 1:50–2:20 — What makes Allocard different

### Visual

Use the delegation tree as the main visual. Highlight the company, employee, and agent connections. Cut briefly to email sign-in and a sponsored payment confirmation.

### Voiceover

“An allowance in Allocard is permission to spend, rather than money sent to an employee’s account. The rules are enforced by contracts on NEAR, including limits passed down to agents.

Employees sign in with email or Google. Privy creates their wallet, and meta-transactions let the relayer pay gas for supported actions. The team gets the controls without needing to handle every payment itself.”

### On-screen text

Show these one at a time:

- **Funds stay in the treasury**
- **Rules enforced on NEAR**
- **Email sign-in · Sponsored payments**

## 2:20–2:30 — Next step

### Visual

End on the dashboard, then the Allocard logo and URL.

### Voiceover

“Try Allocard at allocard.xyz. Bring two email accounts to experience both sides: company control and employee spending.”

### On-screen text

**Try Allocard → allocard.xyz**

Two emails · Separate browser profiles · NEAR testnet

## Recording notes

- Use real app footage for the feature moments. Prepare the accounts, allowances, report, and recipient before recording.
- Keep a small **NEAR testnet demo** label visible during app footage.
- Cut loading time. Keep the result of each action visible long enough to understand it.
- Use close-ups for policy text, allowance amounts, and payment results.
- Keep captions readable without sound.
- Show the research workflow separately from payment; the current agent does not purchase or spend treasury funds.
- Describe sponsorship for supported actions. Some actions still use direct transactions.
- Leave Aurora Intents out of this recording because it cannot fund the testnet treasury.
